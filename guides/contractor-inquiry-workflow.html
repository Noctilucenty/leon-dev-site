What happens after a contractor quote request?

A useful inquiry path does five things: captures enough information to route the job, confirms receipt, assigns a person, follows up with clear stop rules, and records what happened. Use this page to inspect your current path from a customer's phone.

A five-step handoff

The form is the start of the workflow.

  1. 01

    Capture

    Ask for the job type, service area, contact route, and only the details needed to decide who should respond.

  2. 02

    Acknowledge

    Confirm that the request arrived. State the response expectation the business can actually meet; an automatic receipt is not a human reply.

  3. 03

    Route

    Send the inquiry to one monitored inbox, CRM, or shared sheet. Avoid a hidden mailbox that nobody owns.

  4. 04

    Assign and follow up

    Name the person responsible. If reminders are appropriate, limit them and stop when the person replies or opts out.

  5. 05

    Record the result

    Keep distinct statuses for received, acknowledged, assigned, replied, booked, and closed. Do not count a form receipt as a booked estimate.

Eight checks from a customer's phone

Can your current site answer yes?

01

Service fit

Can a visitor tell what work you do and whether their job is in your service area?

02

One estimate action

Is the primary quote or estimate action obvious without opening a menu or zooming?

03

Reasonable intake

Does the form collect enough to route the job without turning the first contact into a long application?

04

Visible receipt

After submission, does the visitor see a clear accepted or failed state rather than an endless spinner?

05

Monitored delivery

Can the team name the exact inbox, CRM, or sheet where every accepted request appears?

06

Named owner

Does each new request have a person responsible for the next human action?

07

Stop rules

Do automated reminders stop on reply or opt-out, with consent and provider requirements documented?

08

Honest measurement

Can you separate received requests from replies, booked estimates, held appointments, and won work?

What the workflow can improve

A clearer path and a reviewable handoff.

  • fewer unanswered questions at the first contact
  • a consistent place for accepted requests to arrive
  • clear responsibility for the next human action
  • event states that can be checked instead of guessed

Claim boundary

What the workflow cannot guarantee

It does not create demand, repair weak pricing, qualify every visitor, or guarantee leads, bookings, revenue, or won jobs. Traffic, service fit, availability, and the team's sales process still matter.
