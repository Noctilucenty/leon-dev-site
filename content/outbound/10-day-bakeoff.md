# Ten-day three-wedge bakeoff

**Status: DRAFT — UNSENT**

**NO SEND AUTHORIZATION.** This is an offline experiment design. It does not
record outreach activity and does not authorize a message or account action.

## Objective

Select one narrow market by evidence from owner conversations, not by enthusiasm,
post views, or email opens. Keep count, research effort, cadence, sender, and
qualification standard comparable across all three wedges.

## Wedge hypotheses

<!-- wedge: home-services -->
### A. Home services (`hs`)

- Target: owner-operated contractor or home-service company with visible demand
  and a public website.
- Observable trigger: mobile quote action is hard to find, the only next step is
  a call, or the follow-up path is unclear.
- Problem hypothesis: a new inquiry may depend on someone seeing and remembering
  to follow up.
- First offer: map the inquiry-to-follow-up path and recommend the smallest useful
  repair, integration, or workflow.
- Risk: there is not yet a named vertical case study, so do not imply one.

<!-- wedge: auto-repair -->
### B. Auto repair (`ar`)

- Target: independent repair shop with active reviews and a public website.
- Observable trigger: appointments require a call, status updates appear manual,
  or the mobile service-request path is unclear.
- Problem hypothesis: booking and routine status questions may consume front-desk
  time.
- First offer: map booking, intake, and status communication around the shop's
  existing tools.
- Risk: do not promise integration until the current shop system and its API or
  export options are verified.

<!-- wedge: restaurant-food -->
### C. Restaurants and food operations (`rf`)

- Target: independent restaurant, food hall, ghost kitchen, or multi-brand food
  operation with public ordering information.
- Observable trigger: ordering leaves the owned site, menus disagree, or several
  brands require separate carts.
- Problem hypothesis: the current ordering path may create operational friction
  or separate the business from customer data.
- First offer: map the current order path and compare a smaller integration with
  a custom build.
- Proof allowed: Leon built one cart and checkout that split orders and accounting
  across 22 businesses. Do not claim a financial result that was not measured.

## Comparable sample

- 25 accounts per wedge; 75 total.
- Use the same public-data research limit per account.
- Require one specific, current observation before a record enters the test.
- Exclude businesses without an identifiable business contact channel, businesses
  already on the suppression list, and any target requiring impersonation or
  gated/private data to diagnose.
- Do not encode the business or person's identity in the tracking URL.

## Ten-day sequence

| Day | Work | Evidence recorded |
|---|---|---|
| D01 | Freeze the three hypotheses, rubric, and message structure. | Version and decision criteria. |
| D02 | Research the first half of each wedge evenly. | Current public URL and one observation per account. |
| D03 | Finish 25 qualified records per wedge; reject weak records. | 75 comparable records or a documented shortfall. |
| D04 | Prepare first-touch drafts for 15 home-service records. | Human-reviewed draft count only. |
| D05 | Prepare first-touch drafts for 15 auto-repair records. | Human-reviewed draft count only. |
| D06 | Prepare first-touch drafts for 15 restaurant/food records. | Human-reviewed draft count only. |
| D07 | Prepare the remaining ten first-touch drafts per wedge. | Equal first-touch coverage. |
| D08 | Prepare first follow-ups for the D04–D05 cohorts; hold owner interviews that were invited. | Replies, positive replies, booked and held calls. |
| D09 | Prepare first follow-ups for the D06–D07 cohorts; code objections in the same categories. | Objection and qualification counts. |
| D10 | Score the wedges and select, extend, or stop the experiment. | Decision memo using the table below. |

This ten-day decision uses the first two touches and real conversations. It does
not cancel already-started compliant follow-up. A selected wedge still completes
the four-touch sequence; losing wedges receive no new records while existing
threads close politely.

## Scorecard

| Metric | Weight | Definition |
|---|---:|---|
| Qualified positive replies | 3 | Recipient describes a relevant problem, asks for the diagnostic, or requests a conversation. |
| Held qualified calls | 3 | Meeting happened and reached the qualification threshold. |
| Observable problem density | 2 | Share of researched accounts with a real, specific trigger. |
| Credible proof fit | 2 | Existing work can support the claim without stretching it. |
| Economic fit | 2 | The problem can rationally support the current canonical offer. |
| Delivery fit | 1 | The likely work is within Leon's capacity and controllable scope. |
| Negative signal | -3 | Complaint, platform warning, misleading assumption, or repeated no-fit response. |

Do not score opens, impressions, likes, profile views, or raw replies that only
say no. Record small sample sizes beside every percentage.

## Decision

- Select: one wedge leads on qualified conversations and has no unresolved safety
  or delivery problem.
- Extend: the top two are close or the samples are too small; add ten comparable
  accounts to each, not a new message and a new market simultaneously.
- Reframe: fewer than three qualified positive replies after all 75 records have
  had two careful touches. Change the problem or proof before adding volume.
- Stop: any systemic complaint, policy warning, inaccurate claim, or inability to
  identify a lawful and respectful contact path.
