## Community rules

- A community group receives a useful process post, not a disguised rate card.
- An ad/classified group may receive a direct offer only when its current rules
  explicitly allow services.
- One group, one language, one manually reviewed draft. Never use bulk cross-post.
- Remove the link when self-promotion is not allowed. Never move a public question
  into DMs unless the member asks.
- Do not promise more customers, higher rankings, fewer no-shows, or savings
  without customer-specific evidence.
- Images remain price-free. Do not bake a figure into an image that cannot be
  updated with the site's canonical prices.

## Partner targets

- Bookkeepers, accountants, and fractional finance operators.
- Local IT support and managed-service providers.
- Brand designers, photographers, copywriters, and small marketing firms.
- POS, CRM, payment, telecom, and operations consultants.
- Commercial insurance, business-law, and business-broker relationships where
  introductions are appropriate and professional rules permit them.
- Chambers, merchant associations, SBDC advisors, and community organizers.

The first ask is a reversible test: one technical triage or one educational
clinic. Do not promise exclusivity, a referral payment, white-label delivery, or
access to client data until terms, professional rules, and customer permission
are documented.

## In-person partner opener

> What technical request do clients keep bringing you that you do not want to
> staff internally? I may be able to diagnose those cases and send the client
> back with a clear next step. I would rather test one handoff than propose a
> broad partnership before we know how each other works.

## Twenty-minute partner agenda

1. Which client problems repeatedly fall outside the partner's scope?
2. What information may be shared, by whom, and only with whose permission?
3. Which work stays with the partner and which work could move to Leon?
4. How quickly must each party acknowledge an introduction?
5. What does a good first test look like?
6. Confirm that there is no exclusivity, fee, or customer-data access unless a
   later written agreement says otherwise.

## Permission-based handoff

> [CLIENT] has agreed that I may introduce you. [CLIENT], this is Leon; Leon,
> this is [CLIENT CONTACT]. The issue, in the client's words, is [VERBATIM OR
> APPROVED SUMMARY]. I am not sending credentials or private records. Please
> keep me [COPIED / NOT COPIED] as agreed. Leon will first determine whether a
> small repair, an existing tool, or a scoped build is the sensible next step.

## Referral follow-through

- Acknowledge the introduction within one business day.
- Answer the client's immediate question before offering a call.
- Never add the introduced person to a marketing sequence.
- Send the partner only the status the client authorized; do not disclose scope,
  credentials, or business data by default.
- After a successful handoff, ask the partner what made the process easy or hard.
